The 72-Hour Fast

"Don't fast harder. Fast smarter."

The Philosophy

A 72-hour fast is a structured period without caloric intake designed to improve metabolic flexibility, reduce insulin exposure, and allow your body to transition from using dietary glucose to stored fat and ketones.

This manual is written for healthy first-time fasters who want a safe, evidence-informed approach. It is not a detox challenge, starvation protocol, or endurance contest.

Before You Begin

Who should not do a 72-hour fast?

Do not attempt this fast without medical guidance if you:

  • Are pregnant or breastfeeding
  • Have type 1 diabetes or use glucose-lowering medication
  • Have a history of an eating disorder
  • Are underweight or malnourished
  • Have significant kidney, liver, or heart disease

If you're unsure, consult your healthcare professional first.

Your preparation checklist

Complete this 24 hours before starting:

  • Eat a balanced final meal
  • Drink 2–3 L of water during the day
  • Sleep 7–9 hours
  • Buy electrolyte salt
  • Prepare chicken broth for Day 4
  • Set your exact finish time

The 72-Hour Timeline

Hours 0–12 — The Transition

Inside your body:
  • Digestion continues
  • Insulin gradually declines
  • Hunger is mostly habitual
What you'll probably feel:
  • Normal
  • Thinking about food at meal times
  • Mild cravings

Coach's reminder: You're hungry because your body expects a meal, not because you're starving.

Hours 12–24 — The Switch Begins

This is where fasting starts becoming noticeable.

Inside your body:
  • Liver glycogen begins falling
  • Fat release increases
  • Ketone production begins rising

Hours 24–36 — Entering Extended Fasting

For many people, this is the hardest psychological phase.

Your metabolism:
  • Fat becomes the primary fuel source
  • Ketones become increasingly important
  • Hunger often becomes less frequent
  • Energy becomes steadier
Stay busy:
  • Read a book or listen to a podcast
  • Work on a hobby or creative project

Hours 36–48 — Deep Fasting

This is where prolonged fasting becomes biologically interesting.

What research supports:
  • Very low insulin
  • Increased fat oxidation
  • Elevated ketone production
What we don't know precisely: Claims like "autophagy peaks at 48 hours" or "stem cells activate at hour 60" go beyond current human evidence.

Hours 48–60 — The Psychological Peak

Many people experience surprising mental clarity. Others feel tired. Both are normal.

Continue only if:
  • You can function normally
  • You're well hydrated
  • No red-flag symptoms are present

Breaking the Fast (72 Hours)

This is a graduated protein re-entry: broth first, then small staged additions every hour. Don't overload a gut that's had nothing solid for 3 days.

Phase 1 — Broth (Hour 0)

  • 1 cup chicken broth
  • Wait, then a second cup a little later

Sip slowly. This resets fluid and sodium before anything else is asked of your gut.

Homemade Chicken Broth

Ingredients:
  • 1 chicken carcass or 500 g chicken bones
  • 2 carrots, 2 celery stalks, 1 onion
  • Handful parsley, 2 L water, 1 tsp sea salt
  • Optional: fresh ginger
Method:
  1. Add everything to a pot
  2. Simmer gently for 3–4 hours
  3. Strain the liquid & store in glass jars

Phase 3 — Bridge Snack

  • Small bowl of Greek yogurt with natural honey

Good for gut bacteria and another gentle protein/carb combo.

Phase 4 — Dinner (Same Day)

  • Chicken or beef
  • Some light carbs
  • Keep the meal small — think 60–70% full, not a feast

The Science (Simplified)

What is well established?

What is promising but still being researched?

  • Magnitude of autophagy in humans
  • Exact timing of cellular recycling
  • Long-term longevity effects
  • Stem-cell regeneration timing

This is exciting science — but it should be presented honestly.

Myth vs Reality

MythReality
"72 hours detoxes the body."Your liver and kidneys detox continuously.
"Autophagy starts exactly at 24h."Human timing varies and isn't precisely measurable.
"Coffee ruins the fast."Black coffee has negligible calories.
"Longer is always better."More isn't automatically healthier.
"Break the fast with a feast."Large meals often cause discomfort.

Final Words

What you accomplished: A successful 72-hour fast is not measured by how much you suffered.

It is measured by your ability to:

  • Prepare properly & Stay hydrated & Maintain electrolyte balance
  • Listen to your body & Finish at your planned endpoint
  • Reintroduce nutrition intelligently

The fast is only half the practice. The re-nutrition is the other half.

This manual is designed to be repeated, refined, and respected — not to encourage pushing beyond your body's limits. Stay calm and trust the process.
