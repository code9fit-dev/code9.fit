What is a 72-Hour Fast?

Understanding extended fasting and its potential benefits

The Basics

A 72-hour fast is a continuous period of 3 days without caloric intake. You drink water, electrolytes, and non-caloric beverages, but consume no food. It's a structured metabolic intervention used by athletes, health professionals, and individuals seeking metabolic flexibility.

Why Do a 72-Hour Fast?

The potential benefits include:

  • Metabolic Adaptation: Your body transitions from using glucose to fat as its primary fuel source
  • Insulin Sensitivity: Prolonged fasting helps reset insulin signaling and improve glucose management
  • Mental Clarity: Many report improved focus and cognitive function during extended fasts
  • Cellular Health: Research suggests fasting may support cellular repair processes
  • Metabolic Flexibility: Training your body to function efficiently without constant food intake

Important Disclaimer

This guide is educational. Extended fasting is not appropriate for everyone. Consult with a healthcare provider before attempting a 72-hour fast, especially if you have a medical condition, take medications, are pregnant, breastfeeding, or have a history of disordered eating.

Before You Begin

Medical considerations and preparation

Who Should Not Fast

Do not attempt a 72-hour fast without medical clearance if you:

  • Are pregnant or breastfeeding
  • Have Type 1 or Type 2 diabetes (unless under medical supervision)
  • Have a history of eating disorders
  • Are underweight or malnourished
  • Take medications that require food intake
  • Have heart disease or cardiac arrhythmias
  • Have kidney or liver disease

Who Should Consult a Healthcare Provider

If you fall into any of these categories, seek medical guidance before fasting:

  • Age over 65
  • Autoimmune conditions
  • History of low blood pressure
  • Taking blood pressure or blood sugar medications
  • Recovering from illness or surgery

Pre-Fast Preparation (24 Hours Before)

Your Preparation Checklist

  • Complete a normal, balanced final meal
  • Hydrate well—drink 2–3 liters of water throughout the day
  • Get 7–9 hours of quality sleep the night before
  • Reduce caffeine intake gradually (avoid withdrawal)
  • Have electrolyte solution ready (sodium, potassium, magnesium)
  • Prepare bone broth for breaking the fast
  • Set your start and end times clearly
  • Inform someone you trust about your intention

What to Gather

Beverages

Water, herbal tea (unsweetened, camomile, rooibos), black coffee (optional, limit to 1 cup)

Electrolytes

Sodium (salt), potassium (coconut water or supplement), magnesium. Consider a quality electrolyte supplement.

Refeeding

Bone broth, light broths, easily digestible proteins for breaking the fast

Daily Hydration & Electrolyte Protocol

Proper hydration and electrolyte balance are critical for safety and comfort during a 72-hour fast.

Water Intake

  • Aim for 2–3 liters per day (8–12 cups)
  • Drink consistently throughout the day—don't chug large amounts at once
  • Increase intake if exercising or if the environment is warm
  • Stop heavy water drinking 1–2 hours before bed to improve sleep

Electrolyte Guidelines

Sodium (Salt)

  • 1–2 teaspoons per day
  • Critical for preventing headaches and fatigue
  • Use sea salt or pink salt
  • Add to water or consume with water

Potassium & Magnesium

  • Potassium: 1000–2000 mg daily (coconut water)
  • Magnesium: 300–400 mg daily
  • Use a quality electrolyte supplement if possible
  • Helps with muscle function and sleep

Beverages You Can Consume

Allowed: Water, herbal tea (unsweetened, camomile, rooibos), black coffee (limit to 1 cup), bone broth (optional—some count this as breaking fast), electrolyte water

Avoid: Artificial sweeteners, diet drinks, juice, milk, sports drinks with calories, alcohol

Training Guidance

This is not the time to test performance. Walking, mobility and gentle stretching are reasonable for many healthy adults. Heavy lifting, HIIT, long endurance sessions and maximal efforts are better postponed.

Movement During the Fast

Recommended Avoid
Walking (20–30 min, daily) High-intensity training (HIIT, CrossFit)
Gentle stretching Heavy lifting (>80% 1RM)
Mobility work Long-distance running
Yoga or Pilates (gentle) Intense competitive sports
Zone 1 cycling (very easy) Any activity that leaves you exhausted

The goal is circulation and light movement—not performance. Intense exercise depletes glycogen and stresses an already-challenged system.

The 72-Hour Timeline

What to expect during each phase

Understanding what happens in your body during each phase helps you recognize normal experiences and stay committed to the process.

Hours 0–12: The Transition Phase

What's happening: Digestion continues. Insulin gradually declines. Your body hasn't yet shifted to fat-burning mode—it's still using available glucose.

What you'll feel: Relatively normal. Possible habitual hunger at meal times. Light cravings are common and expected.

Your job: Stay busy, hydrate well, and remind yourself that hunger is often habitual, not physical need.

Hours 12–24: The Switch Begins

What's happening: Liver glycogen depletes. Ketone production begins. Fat mobilization accelerates. This is where fasting becomes noticeable.

What you'll feel: Increased hunger waves, possible mild fatigue, slight irritability. Cold sensitivity is common. Some people report mental clarity.

Your job: Consume electrolytes, drink water consistently, take a gentle walk. Movement helps metabolic adaptation.

Hours 24–36: Entering Deep Fasting

What's happening: Full ketosis established. Fat is now the primary fuel. Hunger often decreases. Insulin is very low.

What you'll feel: For many, this is the hardest phase psychologically—but many also report the best mental clarity. Energy can fluctuate.

Your job: Keep yourself occupied. Read, work on projects, or rest. Stay hydrated and electrolyte-balanced. Gentle movement only.

Hours 36–48: Deep Metabolic State

What's happening: Continued ketosis and fat oxidation. Growth hormone may increase. Metabolic adaptation is significant.

What you'll feel: Many report surprising energy and clarity. Others feel tired. Both are normal responses.

Your job: Continue normal daily activities if energy permits. Rest if needed. Maintain hydration and electrolytes.

Hours 48–60: The Psychological Peak

What's happening: You're past the halfway point. Metabolically, you're in a deep fasted state. This is often the easiest phase mentally.

What you'll feel: Mental clarity common. Hunger usually suppressed. Some fatigue possible if not eating well before the fast.

Your job: You're on the downhill slope. Stay consistent. Continue protocols. Begin mentally preparing for refeeding.

Hours 60–72: The Final Stretch

What's happening: Final hours of fasting. Your body is fully adapted. Water and electrolyte needs remain high.

What you'll feel: Anticipation of the end. Possible increased hunger as you anticipate food. This is normal.

Your job: Finish strong. Prepare your refeeding meal. Stay hydrated. Plan your break-fast carefully.

Sleep Protocol

Sleep quality directly impacts how well you manage the fast. Prioritize rest.

60 Minutes Before Bed

  • Dim all lights or wear blue-light blocking glasses
  • Stop using screens and devices
  • Drink chamomile tea
  • Practice deep breathing or gentle stretching
  • Journal or read (offline) to calm your mind

Optional Sleep Support

Consider these if you typically use them (don't start new supplements):

  • Magnesium glycinate: 300–400 mg (helps relaxation and muscle recovery)
  • Chamomile tea: 1 cup (mild relaxation)
  • Ashwagandha: 300–600 mg (if tolerated; reduces stress)

Avoid

  • Melatonin (can cause dependency)
  • Alcohol (disrupts sleep quality and dehydrates)
  • Screens or stimulating content
  • Heavy exercise within 4 hours of bedtime
⚠ Stop the Fast Immediately If: You experience fainting, chest pain, severe confusion, or any symptom that concerns you. There is no award for finishing if your body signals distress. Safety always comes first.

Breaking the Fast

Critical refeeding protocol

How you break your fast is as important as the fast itself. A 72-hour fast dramatically increases intestinal sensitivity. Refeeding must be gradual and thoughtful to avoid digestive distress and maximize results.

The Refeeding Principle: After 72 hours of fasting, your digestive system is essentially dormant. Introduce food gradually—large or heavy meals can cause bloating, cramping, or nausea. Think of your gut as needing to "wake up" slowly.

Hour 0: Bone Broth Phase

  • Drink 1–2 cups of warm bone broth slowly
  • This reintroduces salt, minerals, and collagen without overwhelming your system
  • Sip slowly over 20–30 minutes
  • Wait 1–2 hours before consuming anything else

Hours 1–3: Light Broth Continuation

  • Additional 1–2 cups of light broth or vegetable broth
  • Can add a pinch of sea salt
  • Continue sipping slowly
  • Monitor for any digestive discomfort

Hour 3+: Gentle Protein Re-Entry

  • Small portions of easily digestible protein
  • Options: soft-boiled eggs, white fish, chicken broth with shredded chicken, plain yogurt
  • Portion size: 2–3 oz (palm-sized)
  • Eat slowly and chew thoroughly

Hour 6+: Return to Normal

  • If digestion feels stable, add vegetables and healthy fats
  • Focus on whole, unprocessed foods
  • Avoid sugar, processed foods, and excessive fiber initially
  • Remain hydrated

Sample Refeeding Timeline

  • Hour 0: 1 cup bone broth
  • Hour 1.5: 1 cup bone broth + pinch of salt
  • Hour 3: 3 oz poached chicken + 1/2 cup vegetables broth
  • Hour 5: 4 oz white fish + roasted vegetables
  • Hour 8+: Normal, balanced meal

What to Avoid When Breaking the Fast

  • Large portions of food
  • High-fiber foods initially (raw vegetables, whole grains, beans)
  • Sugary foods and refined carbohydrates
  • Fried or heavy foods
  • Caffeine on an empty stomach (wait until you've eaten)
  • Alcohol

After the Fast: What to Expect

Days 1–3 Post-Fast

Your body is readjusting to digestion and nutrient absorption. This is normal:

  • Energy levels may dip as your body normalizes insulin and glucose
  • Digestion may take time to fully normalize
  • Appetite may increase significantly (this is normal adaptation)
  • Continue prioritizing whole foods and hydration
  • Avoid over-restricting calories; let hunger guide you within reason

Days 4–7 Post-Fast

  • Energy typically returns to baseline or improves
  • Mental clarity often remains heightened
  • Digestion normalizes
  • Return to regular training if desired

Key Takeaways

  • Preparation matters: Plan your fast carefully and prepare your environment
  • Hydration and electrolytes are non-negotiable: They're the difference between a smooth experience and a difficult one
  • Breaking the fast is as important as the fast: Rush it and you negate benefits and risk discomfort
  • This is a tool, not a punishment: Use fasting intentionally as part of a sustainable health practice
